---
title: "Sunday, Philadelphia"
date: 2026-08-02
place: "Philadelphia"
note: "Written in a hotel room in August 2026, a few weeks after moving home to Baton Rouge from a year in Bangkok, with coffee carried from Pittsburgh. Patrick Kavanagh is the Irish poet who argued that a parish is enough."
---
A single car
goes slowly by.
A thin bronze
lies over the rowhouse roofs.
I am up early
with jet lag and nerves
in a room
that keeps no memory of me.

Coffee I carried
from Giant Eagle,
which has not changed
in thirty years,
maybe more.
I walk past
Benjamin Franklin High School
and wonder
what he would be proud of,
or ashamed of.

At home
they are still sleeping.
Our house,
cleaner by the day,
holds them.
I miss my home.
I miss being home
with them.

Kavanagh said
a small place was enough.
He was right,
and he was lonely
when he wrote it.
A man can love a street
he has walked twice
and still be counting
the miles back.

I walk toward William Penn
on top of the city.
He lived here
four years.
Prison, a stroke, debt.
He is still up there.
