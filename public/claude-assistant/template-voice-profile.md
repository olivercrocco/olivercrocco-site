# Voice profile: [your name]

Notes for you (delete this section when you finish): a voice profile tells Claude how you write, so that drafts in your name need less fixing. It is optional, and worth building only once you find yourself rewriting the same kinds of drafts. Save the finished file as ~/.claude/voice-profile.md and point to it from your global CLAUDE.md.

The easiest way to build it is to be interviewed. Paste this to Claude:

"Interview me about how I write, one question at a time, about 25 questions, covering the sections in Templates/voice-profile.md. Then read the writing samples I give you and draft my voice profile in that format. Quote my answers where you can rather than paraphrasing them."

Then give it three to five pieces of your own writing in different registers (an email to a colleague, a formal memo, something public), and revise the draft it produces. A profile improves most from before-and-after pairs: each time you fix one of Claude's drafts, add the pair to the last section with a line on what changed.

A caution from experience: a profile gets you most of the way, but the last part of a voice is taste and does not fully transfer. For anything that matters, have Claude check your draft rather than write it.
